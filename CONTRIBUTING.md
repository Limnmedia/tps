# Contributing to TPS

Thank you for taking an interest in the Three Point System. TPS is an evolving, implementation-independent specification developed by LIMNMEDIA.

## What to Contribute

Helpful contributions include:

- Technical corrections and clear explanations.
- Real-world camera, stop-motion, and VFX use cases.
- Independent validation experiments and repeatability observations.
- Relevant academic, historical, and production references.
- Reviews of terminology, assumptions, and mathematical reasoning.
- Reports from implementing or testing TPS in different environments.

## Keep the Distinctions Clear

Please distinguish between:

- What can be observed.
- What a TPS term means.
- What is mathematically established.
- What TPS requires or assumes.
- What is proposed for research.
- What has been experimentally validated.

TPS matches views. It does not claim to solve for a unique camera pose or reproduce an entire scene from three points.

## Proposing Changes

Before opening an issue or pull request:

1. Read the [README](README.md) and [TPS Specification](SPECIFICATION.md).
2. Check the relevant definitions, geometry, constraints, and research documents.
3. Explain the problem or observation clearly.
4. Identify whether the proposal is editorial, mathematical, experimental, or specification-related.
5. Include sources, measurements, or reproducible steps where applicable.

Please do not silently change approved technical wording to resolve a disagreement. Raise the issue or document the proposed change in the research material first.

## Pull Requests

Keep pull requests focused. Describe:

- What changed.
- Why it changed.
- Which documents are affected.
- What checks were performed.
- Any unresolved questions or limitations.

Do not include application code, generated files, private production material, or source material that cannot be redistributed.

## Code of Discussion

Be practical, precise, and welcoming. TPS is intended to be useful to artists and technicians as well as engineers and researchers. Disagreement is welcome when it is specific, evidence-based, and respectful.
